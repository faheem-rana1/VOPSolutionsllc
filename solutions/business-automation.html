01 / Understand

Start with one repeatable process.

Describe what starts the work, who handles it and where it gets stuck. Workflow automation should follow a clear business need, not add another tool to manage.

  • Map recurring tasks and their handoffs.
  • Identify document processing steps.
  • Separate routine decisions from work needing review.
02 / Connect

Move requests between people and systems.

Build around the systems your team uses and the decisions each person needs to make.

  • Microsoft Power Automate for recurring workflows.
  • API integration to connect information across systems.
  • Approval workflows and automated notifications to move requests forward.
03 / Improve

Spend less time preparing the next update.

Extend a clear workflow to the information people need afterward. Keep the purpose and review steps visible as automation grows.

  • Reporting automation for recurring updates.
  • AI-assisted business processes for suitable tasks.
  • Review the workflow as business needs change.

Before you automate

Do we need AI for every workflow?

No. A defined approval or notification process may call for straightforward automation. AI-assisted steps should have a specific purpose.

What should we bring to the conversation?

Choose one recurring task. Describe its inputs, the tools involved, who approves the work and what a useful result looks like.

Which task keeps coming back?

Start with the repeated work you would most like to simplify.
